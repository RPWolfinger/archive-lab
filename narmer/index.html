What am I looking at?

The Narmer Palette (c. 3100 BCE)

A carved stone palette about 64 cm tall that shows King Narmer defeating his enemies. It is one of the earliest objects to name an Egyptian king and to show him wearing the crowns of both Upper and Lower Egypt.

Think Like a Historian

  • Why is the king drawn so much larger than everyone else?
  • The king wears a different crown on each side. What might the two crowns mean?
  • Who do you think was meant to see this palette, and what message were they meant to take away?
  • Is this palette evidence that one war united Egypt, or is it propaganda? What other evidence would you want?

How to use the Archive Lab

  • Drag the palette to turn it. Scroll or pinch to zoom.
  • Click a label, or click the palette itself, to learn about that part.
  • Desk buttons: spin, zoom, labels on/off, real size, and this help.
  • No 3D scan of the palette is free to use, so this model was built from public-domain photographs of the original: its outline, colour and carving come from the photos, and its thickness is approximate.
